# Load Posting Fields, Explained

A load board posting packs a lot into one line. This page walks through each common field and what it should make you ask before you pick up the phone. It is written for owner-operators and small fleets in the US.

## Origin and destination
City, state and sometimes a zip. The zip matters: a "Chicago" pickup can be 40 miles from Chicago. Look it up before you price it.

## Pickup date and window
A date alone is not an appointment. Ask whether it is a firm time, a window, or first-come.

## Equipment
Van, reefer, flatbed, step deck, hotshot, power only, box truck. Watch for add-ons in the comments: "tarps", "team", "liftgate", "food grade".

## Length and weight
Often listed as "full" or "partial" plus pounds. A 44,000 lb load in a 53-foot van is a heavy load; know your own legal gross before you call.

## Rate
Many postings show no rate. When one is shown, divide it by loaded miles, then again by total miles including the empty run to the shipper.

## Deadhead (DH-O and DH-D)
Deadhead origin is the empty miles from where you are to the pickup; deadhead destination is how far the drop is from where you want to end up. These are unpaid miles, so [figure the empty miles to the pickup](https://truckleap.com/deadhead-miles-calculator) before you agree to anything.

## Age of the posting
An old posting may already be covered. Call fast or move on.

## Broker credit and days to pay
Check the broker's credit score and average days to pay on the board, and confirm quick-pay terms before booking.

## Comments
Read every word. "Driver assist", "lumper", "no TONU" and "multi-stop" all change what the load is worth.
